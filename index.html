Why

A high score means little if the test scans were seen in training.

The public MRI dataset hides 704 near-duplicate pairs, and 272 groups of images leak across its original train, validation and test split. Models trained on it can look better than they are.

NeuroScan AI finds the duplicates with file hashes and perceptual hashes, groups them, and splits by group. No scan ever crosses a boundary, and the test set is evaluated once.

Pipeline

From a raw folder to a heatmap, in six steps.

01

Discover

Find every image and the source it was augmented from.

02

Find duplicates

Compare file hashes and perceptual hashes, then cluster the matches.

03

Split by group

A 70 / 15 / 15 split that keeps each cluster in one place.

04

Train

A custom CNN against a fine-tuned EfficientNetB0, both picked on validation macro F1.

05

Evaluate once

Score the held-out test set a single time.

06

Explain

Questions

What people ask first.

Can I use it to diagnose a scan?

No. It is a research project, not a medical device, and it has not been validated for clinical use.

What is data leakage?

When near-identical images land in both training and test sets, the test score is inflated. NeuroScan AI splits by duplicate group to prevent it.

Which model does it use?

EfficientNetB0, with its backbone frozen while the classifier head trains. It was chosen on validation macro F1 against a custom four-block CNN.

Why is meningioma the hardest class?

It overlaps visually with glioma and pituitary tumors, and it has the fewest scans of the three tumor types.

Where is the dataset?

It is not in the repository. Put your copy in a folder named DATASET FOR NEROSCAN AI at the project root.
